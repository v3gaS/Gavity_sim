Mass and Gravity

The orange sphere (mass: 92) creates the deepest curve in spacetime. According to Einstein, massive objects don't "pull" other objects - they curve the spacetime around them, and this curvature affects how other objects move.

Spacetime Curvature

The deformation of the grid shows how mass curves spacetime. The greater the mass, the deeper the "well". This is why more massive objects have stronger gravitational effects.

Orbital Motion

When gravity simulation is active, objects follow curved paths. In reality, objects follow the straightest possible path through curved spacetime - what we perceive as orbital motion.

Light Path Bending

Light always follows the shortest path through spacetime. When spacetime is curved by mass, light paths appear to bend - a phenomenon called gravitational lensing. This effect was first confirmed during a solar eclipse in 1919, providing evidence for Einstein's theory.
